### 2. Positive effects

- RPM increased significantly by 15.51%.
- RPS increased significantly by 10.47%.
- Paid CTR increased significantly by 22.14%.

The largest relative improvement was in Paid CTR.

### 3. Negative effects and guardrail risks

- Organic CTR decreased significantly by 47.58%.
- Pages per Session decreased significantly by 4.36%.

These are substantial engagement risks, particularly the decline in Organic CTR. Statistical significance confirms that the observed decreases should not be treated as random variation according to the provided results.

### 4. Trade-offs

The treatment improved paid engagement and revenue-related metrics while organic engagement and session depth deteriorated.

This creates a monetization–engagement trade-off:

- Paid CTR increased while Organic CTR decreased.
- RPM and RPS increased while Pages per Session decreased.

The results show association between the treatment and these metric changes, but they do not establish that one change caused another.

### 5. Hypotheses to investigate

- Hypothesis: the treatment changed the exposure or placement of paid and organic content, increasing paid interactions while reducing organic interactions.
- Hypothesis: the decline in Organic CTR may be related to the lower Pages per Session.
- Hypothesis: monetization gains and engagement losses may vary across platforms.
- Hypothesis: publisher-level effects may be heterogeneous, with some publishers experiencing disproportionately large engagement losses.
- Hypothesis: the observed effects may not be stable over time.

These are hypotheses, not established facts.

### 6. Recommended additional analyses

- Analyze treatment effects by platform. This would show whether monetization gains and guardrail deterioration are consistent across platforms.
- Analyze results by publisher. This would identify heterogeneous effects and publishers with unusually unfavorable trade-offs.
- Analyze results by date. This would test whether the effects persist throughout the experiment or are concentrated in specific periods.
- Compare monetization and guardrail effects within the same platform and publisher segments. This would identify segments where monetization improves with a smaller engagement cost.
- Define acceptable thresholds for Organic CTR and Pages per Session. This would help determine whether the monetization uplift is sufficient to justify the observed guardrail losses.

### 7. Business recommendation

**Additional investigation**

Do not proceed directly to a full rollout. The significant increases in RPM, RPS, and Paid CTR are meaningful, but they are accompanied by a 47.58% decline in Organic CTR and a 4.36% decline in Pages per Session. Further platform-, publisher-, and date-level analysis is needed to determine whether a safer version of the treatment can preserve monetization benefits while limiting engagement damage.
